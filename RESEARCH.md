# Research dossier: British politics, 1938–1951

Compiled for *The Member*. Base source: the English Wikipedia article for each person or event (URL pattern `https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/<Title>`; exact titles given per entry). 56 biographies and 9 event pages were read.

**How to use this.** Each entry gives early life (for Act I and II scenarios), the 1938–51 record (Act III), and scenario seeds. Seeds marked **(GK)** come from general knowledge and were not confirmed by the page read; check them before stating them as fact.

**Reliability notes.**
- Each page was read through an automated summariser. Dates and figures should be re-checked against the article itself before they appear in the epilogue.
- Known conflicts to resolve: the Norway division was on the night of 8 May 1940 (one summary said 7 May); the July 1942 censure vote is usually given as 475–25 (one summary said 477–25); the Beveridge Report is dated November 1942 and reached the public at the start of December.
- The Wikipedia pages for the Schuman Declaration and the NHS Act 1946 say nothing about British parliamentary politics. The Schuman vote (309–296, June 1950) and the Bevan–Morrison hospitals dispute need another source.

## A. Events

**Norway Debate** (`Norway_Debate`). 7–8 May 1940. Government won 281–200; notional majority of 213 fell to 81. About 41 government supporters voted with the Opposition and some 60 abstained. Rebels included Amery, Keyes (in Admiral of the Fleet's uniform), Macmillan, Boothby, Hogg, Profumo, Hore-Belisha, Spears, Nancy Astor. Chamberlain's appeal to his "friends" in the lobby misfired. 9 May: Labour and Liberals would serve under another Conservative, not him. 10 May: Germany attacked in the west; Chamberlain resigned.

**May 1940 War Cabinet crisis** (`May_1940_War_Cabinet_crisis`). 25–28 May. Halifax wanted to explore terms through Mussolini; Churchill refused; Attlee, Greenwood and Sinclair backed Churchill; Chamberlain wavered, then came down for fighting on. 28 May: Churchill addressed the 25 ministers outside the War Cabinet, who backed him. Halifax had considered resigning. Bridges took the minutes; Cadogan and Colville recorded it in diaries.

**Beveridge Report** (`Beveridge_Report`). Kingsley Wood called it financially impracticable and urged delay. February 1943 debate: the government declined to implement at once; 97 Labour MPs, 11 Independents, 9 Liberals, 3 ILP and 1 Communist voted against it. Bevin was furious with the Labour rebels. The Tory Reform Committee (45 MPs) wanted a Ministry of Social Security now. Churchill's March 1943 broadcast offered a four-year plan after the war.

**Military Training Act 1939** (`Military_Training_Act_1939`). Cabinet agreed 27 April; royal assent 26 May. Men of 20 and 21, six months' training. 87% of Labour MPs voted against. First registration 3 June 1939. Superseded on the outbreak of war.

**Munich** (`Munich_Agreement`). Popular at first: cheering crowds, the Palace balcony, over 20,000 letters of thanks, majority support in polls. Churchill called it a total defeat on 5 October. Labour opposed from the start. Division 366–144 with about 30 Conservative abstentions **(GK)**.

**Churchill war ministry** (`Churchill_war_ministry`). 13 May 1940 confidence vote 381–0. Labour's conference opposed continuing the coalition; Labour left 23 May 1945; caretaker government until 26 July.

**Attlee ministry / premiership** (`Attlee_ministry`, `Premiership_of_Clement_Attlee`). 1945: 393 seats, majority 146. US loan December 1945 required convertibility; convertibility began July 1947 and was suspended after five weeks. Fuel crisis, winter 1946–47. 1947: Dalton, then Cripps, tried to replace Attlee with Bevin; Bevin refused. January 1947: secret decision to build the bomb. July 1949: 13,000 troops sent into the London docks. January 1950: recognition of Communist China. 1950: majority of 5. April 1951: Bevan, Wilson and Freeman resigned. October 1951: Labour won more votes and lost.

## B. Camp 1 — The Bulldog

**Winston Churchill** (`Early_life_of_Winston_Churchill`, `Winston_Churchill`, `Winston_Churchill_in_the_Second_World_War`, `Later_life_of_Winston_Churchill`)
- *Early:* b. 30 Nov 1874, Blenheim. Distant parents; closest to his nanny, Mrs Everest. Miserable at his Ascot prep school; better at Hove. Harrow from 1888, barely passing in; good only at English and History. Sandhurst at the third attempt, passed out Dec 1894. Cuba 1895 with Spanish troops. Bangalore 1896–97: taught himself from Gibbon, Darwin, Macaulay. Malakand 1897 as soldier-journalist, first book. Omdurman 1898 with the 21st Lancers; criticised Kitchener's treatment of wounded. Captured on an armoured train near Colenso, Oct 1899; escaped from Pretoria via a mine and a goods train. MP for Oldham, Oct 1900, aged 25.
- *Middle:* maiden speech Feb 1901; attacked Army spending. Crossed to the Liberals 31 May 1904 (free trade; the Aliens Bill). 1908: lost Manchester NW, won Dundee, married. 1910 Tonypandy: held back troops, sent 270 London police. Jan 1911 Sidney Street. Admiralty Oct 1911. Antwerp 1914. Dardanelles failed; resigned 25 Nov 1915. Commanded 6th Royal Scots Fusiliers at Ploegsteert, Jan–May 1916. Munitions 1917. War Office 1919: backed the Whites in Russia. Backed the Black and Tans; helped draft the Irish Treaty. 1922: bought Chartwell, appendix out, lost Dundee. 1924: Epping; Chancellor. 1925: gold at the 1914 parity against Keynes. 1926: edited the *British Gazette*. Jan 1931: left the Shadow Cabinet over India. 13 Dec 1931: knocked down by a car on Fifth Avenue.
- *1938–51:* Admiralty 3 Sept 1939. PM 10 May 1940. Confidence votes Jan 1942 (464–1) and July 1942. Mild heart attack 26 Dec 1941; pneumonia Feb 1943. Bengal famine 1943 (about three million dead); Wavell made Viceroy Oct 1943. Moscow, Oct 1944: the percentages. Lost July 1945. Fulton, 5 Mar 1946. Zurich 1946. Opposed an early handover in India, Mar 1947. Attacked Labour for staying out of the Schuman talks, June 1950. PM again Oct 1951, majority 17.
- *Seeds (GK):* the kicked straw hat at Ascot; the blank Latin paper; the 29-foot fall at Bournemouth; the Empire Theatre partitions; the dislocated shoulder at Bombay; the Mauser at Omdurman; leaving comrades behind at Pretoria; Mers-el-Kébir, July 1940; the 1945 "Gestapo" broadcast; the 1949 stroke; the Epping deselection threat 1938–39; the 1938 bail-out of his finances.

**Brendan Bracken** (`Brendan_Bracken`). b. 1901, Templemore, Tipperary; father a builder, IRB man and GAA founder who died 1904. Ran wild at a Jesuit school; packed off to Australia. In 1920 talked his way into Sedbergh as a 15-year-old Australian orphan. Built a publishing empire (*The Banker*, *The Economist*, the modern *Financial Times*). Attached himself to Churchill in 1923. MP Paddington North 1929 by 528 votes. May 1940: persuaded Churchill to stay silent when Halifax was proposed. PPS 1940–41; Minister of Information 1941–45. Lost his seat 1945; back for Bournemouth that November. *Seed:* the invented self; the courtier who makes the king.

**Frederick Lindemann, "the Prof"** (`Frederick_Lindemann,_1st_Viscount_Cherwell`). b. 1886 Baden-Baden; Berlin physics under Nernst. Farnborough 1915: worked out spin recovery and learned to fly to test it. Oxford chair 1919. At Chartwell over 100 times. 1940: Churchill's scientific adviser; ran the Statistical Branch. Sidelined Tizard. 30 Mar 1942: the "dehousing" paper for area bombing. 1943: advised against diverting shipping to Bengal. Called the V-2 a hoax. *Seed:* the adviser's chart on the PM's desk; whose numbers do you trust.

**Desmond Morton** (`Desmond_Morton_(civil_servant)`). b. 1891. Royal Artillery; shot through the lung at Arras 1917 and served on with the bullet in him; ADC to Haig. SIS in the 1920s. Headed the Industrial Intelligence Centre 1929–39. Lived a mile from Chartwell and walked over the fields to brief Churchill on German rearmament. Personal assistant at No. 10 from 1940, handling Ultra; influence faded as the machine formalised. *Seed:* do you use secrets a civil servant should not be giving you.

**Roger Keyes** (`Roger_Keyes,_1st_Baron_Keyes`). b. 1872. Zeebrugge raid 1918. MP Portsmouth North 1934. Pushed a plan to force Trondheim fjord, April 1940; refused. Spoke in the Norway Debate in full uniform. Liaison to King Leopold, May 1940. First Director of Combined Operations, June 1940; removed Oct 1941. *Seed:* the uniform in the Chamber.

**Leo Amery** (`Leo_Amery`). b. 1873, Gorakhpur; mother of Hungarian Jewish descent. Harrow, Balliol, All Souls. *Times* correspondent in the Boer War; his attack helped end Buller's command. Lost a 1908 by-election by eight votes. Intelligence officer 1914–18; torpedoed. Short of money; took directorships in the 1930s. Stayed seated when the House cheered the Munich flight. 2 Sept 1939: called across the floor for Greenwood to speak for England. Ended his Norway speech with Cromwell's dismissal of the Long Parliament. India Secretary 1940–45, outside the War Cabinet; clashed with Churchill over Gandhi and over famine relief; overruled on grain, Aug 1943. Lost his seat 1945.

**Anthony Eden** (`Anthony_Eden`) — bridges camps 1 and 6. b. 1897, Windlestone, Durham; violent-tempered baronet father. Eton. King's Royal Rifle Corps 1915; MC 1916; youngest brigade major in the Army at 20; two brothers killed. Double First in Persian and Arabic. MP 1923. Foreign Secretary Dec 1935; resigned 20 Feb 1938 over Chamberlain's approach to Mussolini. Led the dissidents the Chief Whip called the "Glamour Boys". Abstained on Munich. Dominions 1939; War Office May–Dec 1940; Foreign Secretary 1940–45. Son Simon lost over Burma, June 1945.

## C. Camp 2 — The Quiet Major

**Clement Attlee** (`Clement_Attlee`, `Premiership_of_Clement_Attlee`)
- *Early:* b. 3 Jan 1883, Putney, seventh of eight; father a Liberal solicitor. Haileybury 1896. University College, Oxford, 1901–04; second in modern history; by his own account an old-fashioned imperialist conservative. Called to the Bar 1906; disliked it. 1907–09 managed the Haileybury boys' club in Stepney; the poverty made him a socialist. ILP 1908. Father died Nov 1908; he gave up law. Secretary to Beatrice Webb; Toynbee Hall; LSE lecturer 1912. Commissioned Sept 1914, aged 31. Gallipoli: among the last off Suvla. Wounded at Hanna, Mesopotamia, April 1916. Major 1917. Mayor of Stepney 1919. MP Limehouse Nov 1922.
- *Middle:* Under-Secretary for War 1924. Simon Commission 1927. Acting leader from Dec 1933; leader Oct–Dec 1935. April 1936: called Chamberlain's budget a war budget. By 1937 Labour backed rearmament. Late 1937: visited British volunteers in Spain.
- *1938–51:* called Munich a victory for brute force. Labour opposed conscription, April 1939. In hospital at the outbreak of war. Lord Privy Seal 12 May 1940; Deputy PM Feb 1942. PM 26 July 1945. See section A for the government's crises.
- *Seeds (GK):* a governess who had earlier taught the young Churchill; caned after Mafeking night at Haileybury; four briefs in three years; cycling round explaining the 1911 Insurance Act; brother Tom a conscientious objector; dysentery at Gallipoli kept him out of the battle that wrecked his battalion; hit by British shrapnel at Hanna; 1926 Stepney electricity committee and a personal damages judgment; holding Limehouse by 551 in 1931; Cripps paying towards his secretary; the Bournemouth telephone call, 10 May 1940; chairing Cabinet when the Congress leaders were arrested, Aug 1942; the long typed letter rebuking Churchill, Jan 1945; Bevin telling him to go straight to the Palace; the September 1947 telephone call to Bevin with Cripps in the room; in St Mary's with an ulcer, April 1951.

**Arthur Greenwood** (`Arthur_Greenwood`). b. 1880, Hunslet, Leeds; father a painter and decorator. Read *The Clarion* as a schoolboy. Leeds BSc; taught economics at Huddersfield. Wartime civil servant in Reconstruction. Head of Labour's research department 1927. Third in the 1935 leadership ballot; deputy leader to 1945. 2 Sept 1939: stood in for the hospitalised Attlee and pressed Chamberlain over Poland. Heavy drinker. War Cabinet May 1940; backed fighting on. June 1941: set up the insurance committee and put Beveridge in the chair. Dropped Feb 1942. Sacked again Sept 1947. *Seed:* the deputy's moment; drink as a health strand.

**James Chuter Ede** (`James_Chuter_Ede`). b. 1882, Epsom; Unitarian grocer's son. Left Cambridge after two years when the money ran out. Elementary schoolteacher; NUT. Regimental Sergeant Major 1914–18. Junior education minister 1940–45; carried the 1944 Education Act with Butler. Home Secretary 1945–51: Police and Fire Acts, British Nationality Act 1948, one person one vote 1948. Opposed abolishing hanging in 1948; confirmed Timothy Evans's sentence in 1950 and later concluded it was wrong. *Seed:* the reprieve file on the Home Secretary's desk.

**A. V. Alexander** (`A._V._Alexander,_1st_Earl_Alexander_of_Hillsborough`). b. 1885, Weston-super-Mare; father died 1886; mother a corset-maker. Left school at 13; council clerk. Baptist. Co-operative movement; MP Sheffield Hillsborough 1922. First Lord of the Admiralty 1940–45, kept out of the War Room. Cabinet Mission to India 1946. Minister of Defence 1946–50; fought his own backbenchers over peacetime national service.

**Jim Griffiths** (`Jim_Griffiths`). b. 1890, Betws, Carmarthenshire; blacksmith's son; no English until five. Down the pit at 13. ILP branch founder 1908; anti-war campaigner 1914–18. Central Labour College 1919–21. President of the South Wales miners 1934–36. MP Llanelli 1936. Led the 97 over Beveridge. Minister of National Insurance 1945–50: Family Allowances, National Insurance Act 1946, Industrial Injuries. Colonial Secretary 1950–51.

## D. Camp 3 — The Man of Business

**Neville Chamberlain** (`Neville_Chamberlain`). b. 1869, Birmingham; Joseph's son, Austen's half-brother. Rugby; Mason College. Six years failing to grow sisal in the Bahamas; his father lost £50,000. Seventeen years running a Birmingham metal firm. Lord Mayor 1915. Director of National Service Dec 1916; resigned Aug 1917 after clashing with Lloyd George. MP at 49. Minister of Health 1924–29: 21 of 25 planned bills passed; Poor Law boards abolished. PM May 1937. Munich 30 Sept 1938. Guarantee to Poland 31 Mar 1939. Resigned 10 May 1940; backed Churchill in the May crisis. Died 9 Nov 1940.

**Lord Halifax** (`Edward_Wood,_1st_Earl_of_Halifax`). b. 1881, no left hand; three elder brothers dead by the time he was nine. Eton, Christ Church, All Souls. Devout Anglo-Catholic. Viceroy 1926–31: the Dominion-status declaration; the pact with Gandhi. Saw Hitler Nov 1937. Foreign Secretary 21 Feb 1938. Opposed the Godesberg terms in Cabinet; hardened after Kristallnacht; drove the Polish guarantee. 9 May 1940: declined the premiership. Late May 1940: pressed for terms via Italy; nearly resigned. Washington from Jan 1941. Son killed in Egypt 1942.

**Samuel Hoare** (`Samuel_Hoare,_1st_Viscount_Templewood`). b. 1880; banking family. Harrow, New College. Intelligence in Petrograd, then Rome, where he paid British subsidies to Mussolini's paper. India Secretary 1931–35: the Government of India Act. Resigned over the Hoare–Laval Pact, 18 Dec 1935. Home Secretary 1937–39; planned penal reform. March 1939: the "golden age" speech days before Prague. Dropped 10 May 1940. Ambassador in Madrid 1940–44, keeping Franco out.

**John Simon** (`John_Simon,_1st_Viscount_Simon`). b. 1873, Moss Side; Congregational minister's son. Fettes; Wadham; Union president. Liberal MP 1906. Resigned as Home Secretary, Jan 1916, against conscription. Simon Commission 1927–30. Led the Liberal Nationals 1931. As Home Secretary declined to ban the 1936 Fascist march (Cable Street). Chancellor 1937–40. 2 Sept 1939: led the ministers' deputation telling Chamberlain to declare war. Lord Chancellor 1940–45; interviewed Hess.

**Kingsley Wood** (`Kingsley_Wood`). b. 1881, Hull; Wesleyan minister's son. Solicitor; insurance specialist. LCC 1911; MP Woolwich West 1918. Air Secretary 1938–40: monthly output from 80 to 546 aircraft; restricted the RAF to leaflets in the Phoney War. May 1940: told Chamberlain to go and told Churchill not to defer to Halifax. Chancellor 1940–43: brought in Keynes; called Beveridge unaffordable. Died 21 Sept 1943, the morning he was to announce PAYE.

**Horace Wilson** (`Horace_Wilson_(civil_servant)`). b. 1882, Bournemouth; furniture dealer's son. Entered the civil service's Second Division 1900; LSE. Permanent Secretary, Labour, 1921. Seconded to the PM 1935. Flew with Chamberlain to Germany; carried a letter alone to Hitler, 26 Sept 1938. Talks with Wohlthat, July 1939. Head of the Civil Service 1939. Told, on Churchill's arrival, not to show his face at No. 10. Retired 1942.

## E. Camp 4 — The Docker

**Ernest Bevin** (`Ernest_Bevin`). b. 1881, Winsford, Somerset; father unknown; mother died 1889. Left school 1892. Farm labourer at eleven; drayman in Bristol. Baptist lay preacher. Bristol dockers' secretary 1910. Built the TGWU, 1922. Lukewarm about the General Strike. Lost Bristol Central 1918 and Gateshead 1931. Minister of Labour 13 May 1940 without a seat; returned unopposed for Wandsworth. Directed manpower; sent 48,000 conscripts down the mines. Foreign Secretary 1945–51: the US loan, Marshall Aid, the Brussels Treaty, NATO. Insisted on a British bomb. Kept the limits on Jewish immigration to Palestine. Stood by Attlee in 1947. Died 14 April 1951. *Seed (GK):* the "dockers' KC" at the 1920 inquiry, with the plate of a docker's dinner.

**George Isaacs** (`George_Isaacs`). b. 1883, Finsbury; Methodist. Printer; general secretary of NATSOPA for forty years from 1909. Mayor of Southwark. Won Gravesend 1923 by 119. Minister of Labour 1945–51: demobilisation. 1948: told the House he did not know who had sent the *Windrush* passengers and hoped others would not follow.

**James Callaghan** (`James_Callaghan`). b. 1912, Portsmouth; father, a naval petty officer, died 1921; charity until a 1924 widow's pension. No money for university. Tax clerk at 17; union official 1936. Navy from 1942 (reserved occupation at first). Selected for Cardiff South by 12 votes to 11. One of 22 Labour rebels against the US loan. Junior Transport minister 1947: zebra crossings, cat's eyes. Backed emergency powers in the dock strikes.

## F. Camp 5 — The Firebrand

**Aneurin Bevan** (`Aneurin_Bevan`). Expelled from Labour March–Dec 1939 with Cripps over the Popular Front. Opposed the 1939 conscription Act. Wartime leader of the left opposition: against censorship and detention without trial, for coal nationalisation and a Second Front. 1944: voted against the party on a defence regulation; kept the whip 71–60. Minister of Health at 47. Rebuked for wearing a lounge suit to a royal banquet. Fought the BMA 1946–48; conceded private practice for consultants. NHS began 5 July 1948. The "vermin" speech, 1948. Passed over for the Foreign Office; Minister of Labour Jan 1951. Resigned 23 April 1951. *Early life re-read 11 Nov 2026 (`Aneurin_Bevan`, verbatim text):* b. 15 Nov 1897, 32 Charles Street, Tredegar; father David, a miner (Baptist); mother Phoebe (née Prothero), a seamstress (Methodist); ten children, four died in infancy and one at eight. Sirhowy Elementary School, left at 14; butcher's boy at 13 for some months; Ty-Trist Colliery, about ten shillings a week; head of the miners' lodge at 19; later Bedwellty, Whitworth and Pochin (a punishment posting). Severe stammer, overcome at the Central Labour College by reciting William Morris. Stood for Tredegar UDC (West Ward) 1919; SWMF scholarship to the Central Labour College 1919–21. Blacklisted by Tredegar Iron and Coal Co. 1921; six weeks as a council labourer; Bedwellty Colliery 1924, closed ten months later. Father died Feb 1925 of pneumoconiosis, uncompensated (not then an industrial disease). Paid lodge official 1926 at £5 a week; Council of Action. Monmouthshire County Council 1928. Tredegar Medical Aid Society; chairman of the Cottage Hospital committee 1929–30. Adopted for Ebbw Vale 1928, displacing Evan Davies; won 1929 (20,000 to 8,000). One of 17 signatories of the Mosley Memorandum; refused the New Party. Married Jennie Lee 1934. Expelled March 1939, readmitted 20 Dec 1939. Kept the whip 71–60 in 1944.

**Shinwell, re-read 11 Nov 2026:** clothing union 1903; Glasgow Trades Council 1906; seamen's organiser May 1911 and the Glasgow seamen's strike from 14 June 1911; British Seafarers' Union 1912–22; stood Linlithgowshire 1918; five months in Calton Jail (Edinburgh) after George Square, January 1919; MP Linlithgowshire 1922; Secretary for Mines 1924 and 1930–31; Seaham 1935 against MacDonald; struck Robert Bower in the Commons, April 1938; refused the Ministry of Food in May 1940.

**Emanuel Shinwell** (`Emanuel_Shinwell`). b. 1884, Spitalfields, eldest of thirteen; Jewish immigrant family; Glasgow. Left school at eleven for tailoring; educated himself in the public library. Seamen's organiser. Five months in Calton Jail after George Square, 1919. Beat Ramsay MacDonald at Seaham, 1935. Refused a junior post in May 1940 as an insult. Fuel and Power 1945: coal nationalised; promised no fuel crisis before the winter of 1946–47. Demoted Oct 1947; replaced by his deputy Gaitskell. Minister of Defence 1950; his rearmament bill helped trigger the health charges.

**Sydney Silverman** (`Sydney_Silverman`). b. 1895, Liverpool; poor Romanian-Jewish draper's son; scholarships. Three prison terms as a conscientious objector. Solicitor for tenants and injured workers. MP Nelson and Colne 1935. Dropped his pacifism over the persecution of Jews. Kept out of office in 1945 for being too far left. 1948: his amendment to suspend hanging passed the Commons and died in the Lords.

**Jennie Lee** (`Jennie_Lee,_Baroness_Lee_of_Asheridge`). b. 1904, Lochgelly, Fife; miner's daughter; grandfather a friend of Keir Hardie. Dux of her school; Edinburgh MA, LLB. Won North Lanark for the ILP in March 1929, the youngest MP, before women her age had the vote. Stood as Independent Labour at Bristol Central, 1943, and lost. Cannock 1945.

**Michael Foot** (`Michael_Foot`). b. 1913, Plymouth; Liberal dynasty. Oxford Union president. A Birkenhead shipping clerk in 1934; Liverpool's poverty turned him socialist. Stood at Monmouth 1935 for disarmament. Rejected for service (asthma). Co-wrote *Guilty Men*, 1940. Edited Beaverbrook's *Evening Standard* at 28. Won Devonport 1945. *Keep Left*, 1947. Edited *Tribune* from 1948.

**Woodrow Wyatt** (`Woodrow_Wyatt`). b. 1918; headmaster's son. Major; Normandy on D+1; nearly court-martialled after a row with a senior officer; posted to India. Aston 1945. Go-between with the Muslim League during the 1946 Cabinet Mission. Keep Left 1947. Junior War Office minister 1951.

## G. Camp 6 — The Middle Way

**Harold Macmillan** (`Harold_Macmillan`). b. 1894; publishing family, crofter great-grandfather. Eton, cut short by illness; Balliol. Grenadier Guards; wounded at Loos 1915 and badly on the Somme, Sept 1916; two years in hospital. Married a Duke's daughter 1920. MP Stockton 1924; lost 1929; back 1931 as a planner. Resigned the whip 1936 over Abyssinia. Voted against Chamberlain, May 1940. Junior posts 1940–42; nearly resigned Nov 1942. Minister Resident in North Africa and Italy from Dec 1942. Burned in a plane crash, Feb 1943. Greece, Dec 1944: the Regency. Lost Stockton 1945; Bromley by-election, Nov 1945. **(GK):** his wife's long affair with Boothby, from 1930.

**Rab Butler** (`Rab_Butler`). b. 1902, Attock; ICS family. Hand crippled in a riding fall at six. Marlborough; Cambridge Union president; a First. Married Courtauld money. MP 1929. Carried the 1935 India Act against Churchill. Foreign Office spokesman in the Commons from 1938: defended Munich; after the Nazi–Soviet Pact, argued against honouring the Polish guarantee. May 1940: wanted Halifax. 17 June 1940: told the Swedish envoy policy would rest on common sense, not bravado; offered to resign. Education 1941: the 1944 Act. 28 March 1944: beaten by one vote on equal pay for women teachers; Churchill reversed it as a confidence issue. The Industrial Charter, 1947.

**Bob Boothby** (`Bob_Boothby`). b. 1900, Edinburgh. Eton, Magdalen. Stockbroker. MP 1924. Met Hitler 1932 and backed rearmament. Junior Food minister May 1940; forced out Oct 1940 for not declaring an interest. RAF staff officer; Free French liaison. Council of Europe delegate 1949.

**Ronald Cartland** (`Ronald_Cartland`). b. 1907; family fortune gone; father killed in France 1918. Charterhouse scholar; no money for university; Conservative Central Office. MP King's Norton 1935 with Chamberlain family backing. Maiden speech attacked neglect of the distressed areas. 2 Aug 1939: accused Chamberlain to his face of dictatorial ideas over the adjournment. Killed 30 May 1940 on the retreat to Dunkirk.

**Quintin Hogg** (`Quintin_Hogg,_Baron_Hailsham_of_St_Marylebone`). b. 1907; Lord Chancellor's son. Eton, Christ Church, All Souls. Won the Oxford by-election, Oct 1938, as the Munich candidate. Voted against Chamberlain, May 1940. Wounded in the knee in the desert, Aug 1941. Wrote a reply to *Guilty Men*, 1945. Forced into the Lords by his father's death, 1950.

**John Profumo** (`John_Profumo`). b. 1915. Harrow, Brasenose. Won Kettering at a by-election, March 1940, as a serving officer and the youngest MP. Voted against Chamberlain weeks later and was savagely abused by the Chief Whip. Normandy on D-Day; brigadier. Lost 1945; Stratford 1950.

## H. Camp 7 — The Planner

**Hugh Dalton** (`Hugh_Dalton`). b. 1887; son of a royal chaplain and tutor to the future George V. Eton; King's, Cambridge, where he turned socialist. Artillery on the Italian front; decorated in the Caporetto retreat. LSE lecturer. Economic Warfare 1940–42: set up SOE; feuds with Wavell and Duff Cooper. Board of Trade 1942–45: coal rationing dropped after a Tory revolt. Chancellor 1945–47: cheap money; the sterling crisis. Resigned Nov 1947 after telling a reporter budget details on the way into the Chamber. Patron of Gaitskell, Jay, Durbin.

**Stafford Cripps** (`Stafford_Cripps`). b. 1889; wealthy; Tory MP father. Winchester; chemistry at UCL. Red Cross driver, then ran a munitions factory. The richest patent barrister of his day; KC 1927. Labour 1930; Socialist League. Expelled 1939. Ambassador in Moscow 1940–42. Leader of the House Feb–Nov 1942, briefly a rival to Churchill; the failed India mission, March 1942. Aircraft Production to 1945. Board of Trade 1945; tried to move Attlee out, 1947. Chancellor Nov 1947–Oct 1950: austerity; devaluation 1949. Resigned ill.

**Hugh Gaitskell** (`Hugh_Gaitskell`). b. 1906; ICS family; Burma childhood; father died 1915. Dragon, Winchester, New College. Backed the strikers in 1926. Taught Nottinghamshire miners for the WEA. Vienna 1934: saw the socialists crushed. Dalton's private secretary 1940–45. Heart trouble March 1945; Leeds South that July. Took over fuel from Shinwell, 1947; ended the basic petrol ration. Led the push for devaluation, July 1949. Chancellor Oct 1950. April 1951 budget: charges on teeth and spectacles.

**Douglas Jay** (`Douglas_Jay`). b. 1907. Winchester, New College, All Souls. Journalist on *The Times*, *The Economist*, the *Daily Herald*. *The Socialist Case* (1937) and its much-quoted line about the gentleman in Whitehall. Wartime civil servant; Dalton's assistant. Battersea North by-election 1946. Treasury minister 1947–51.

**Harold Wilson** (`Harold_Wilson`). b. 1916, Huddersfield; father an industrial chemist out of work for nearly two years from Dec 1930. Grammar school; Jesus College, Oxford; a First. Research assistant to Beveridge. Wartime statistician at Fuel and Power. Ormskirk 1945. President of the Board of Trade at 31; the "bonfire of controls", 1948. Changed sides repeatedly on devaluation. Resigned with Bevan, April 1951.

**Evan Durbin** (`Evan_Durbin`). b. 1906; Baptist minister's son. New College; friend of Gaitskell from the General Strike. LSE. *The Politics of Democratic Socialism*, 1940. Attlee's personal assistant 1942–45. Edmonton 1945. Drowned 3 Sept 1948 off Cornwall saving his daughter.

## I. Camp 8 — The Pioneer

**Ellen Wilkinson** (`Ellen_Wilkinson`). b. 1891, Manchester; cotton worker turned insurance agent; Methodist. Scholarships to Manchester University. ILP at 16; suffrage organiser; union organiser from 1915. Founder member of the Communist Party; left 1924. MP Middlesbrough East 1924. Expelled from Spain 1934. Led the Jarrow March, Oct 1936. Junior minister 1940; ran the shelter programme under Morrison. Backed Morrison against Attlee, 1945, and was still made Minister of Education. Raised the leaving age to 15 for April 1947. Died 6 Feb 1947.

**Eleanor Rathbone** (`Eleanor_Rathbone`). b. 1872; Liverpool shipowning reformers. Somerville, without a degree. Studied dock labour. Suffragist. Independent MP for the Combined English Universities 1929. Founded the Parliamentary Committee on Refugees, 1938. 10 July 1940: attacked the treatment of interned anti-Nazi refugees. 1942: pressed for publicity about the extermination of Jews. Family Allowances Act 1945, paid to mothers. Oct 1945: protested the expulsions of Germans. Died 2 Jan 1946.

**Nancy Astor** (`Nancy_Astor,_Viscountess_Astor`). b. 1879, Virginia. First woman to take her seat, 1919. Tainted by Cliveden and by antisemitic remarks. Voted against Chamberlain in 1940. Rambling by 1943. Pushed into retirement 1945.

**Edith Summerskill** (`Edith_Summerskill`). b. 1901. Doctor. Won Fulham West, 1938. Pressed for women in the Home Guard. Junior Food minister 1945–50; clean milk. Minister of National Insurance 1950–51.

**Megan Lloyd George** (`Megan_Lloyd_George`). b. 1902. Anglesey 1929, first woman MP in Wales. Radical Action; a Welsh Parliament. Deputy Liberal leader 1949. Lost Anglesey 1951.

**Mavis Tate** (`Mavis_Tate`). b. 1893. Conservative MP 1931. Joined the Right Club, May 1939; urged mass internment in 1940; broke with the far right after a breakdown. **Keyes, early (added detail).** b. 4 Oct 1872, Punjab; general's son. HMS *Britannia* 15 July 1885. 1887 HMS *Raleigh*, Cape station; midshipman; corvette *Turquoise* from Zanzibar against the slave trade. 1893 *Beagle*, Pacific. 1898 destroyer *Opossum*, Plymouth; Sept 1898 to China (*Hart*, then *Fame*, Jan 1899). April 1899, New Territories: led a charge to relieve a surrounded British party. June 1900, Peiho: boarded and captured four Chinese destroyers; then with 32 men destroyed the Hsi-cheng fort's guns and magazine. Among the first over the Peking walls to the legations. Commander Nov 1900.

**Amery, early (added detail).** b. 22 Nov 1873, Gorakhpur; Hindi from his ayah; mother moved to England 1877, divorced 1885. Harrow 1887: top of examinations; gymnast; pushed into the school pool by the younger Churchill. Balliol: double first (1894, 1896); Ouseley scholar in Turkish; half-blue. Fellow of All Souls. Languages: Turkish, Bulgarian, Serbian, Hungarian. Boer War for *The Times*: the only correspondent to visit the Boer forces; nearly captured with Churchill; his 1901 articles helped end Buller's command. 1908 Wolverhampton East by-election lost by eight votes. No private means.

**Lindemann, early (added detail).** b. 5 Apr 1886, Baden-Baden; American mother, naturalised German-born father. School in Scotland and Darmstadt; Berlin under Nernst. 1911 Solvay Conference, youngest present. Fled Germany in August 1914 from a tennis tournament to avoid internment. Farnborough 1915–16: spin recovery theory, learned to fly to test it. "Baron Berlin" to detractors.

**Bracken, early (added detail).** b. 15 Feb 1901, Templemore. 1915 bolted from Mungret College (Jesuit), ran up hotel bills; sent to a priest cousin at Echuca, Victoria. 1920 at Sedbergh posed as a 15-year-old Australian bush-fire orphan. Schoolmaster; publisher from 1922 (advertising). Introduced to Churchill by J. L. Garvin, 1923; stabbed in street fighting at the 1924 Westminster Abbey by-election. North Paddington 1929 by 528.

**Morton, early.** b. 13 Nov 1891. Royal Artillery 1911. Shot in the lung at Arras 1917, served on with the bullet in him; MC. ADC to Haig. SIS Section V. (Article silent on school and family.)
