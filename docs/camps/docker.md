**Anchor:** Ernest Bevin. **Divergence:** moderate. **Vector:** order −7, empire +2, state +6, steel +8, loyalty +7, temper +1.

## Act II hinges (1922–1938)

- **1920, the dockers' KC** (spine): the Shaw inquiry and the plate of a docker's dinner. GK; verify.
- **1922, the amalgamation**: build one big union; who gets which office.
- **1926, the General Strike**: lukewarm. Call the dockers out or hold them back. This hinge decides `@movement`.
- **1931, Gateshead** (spine): lose the seat; stay with the union.
- **1935, Brighton**: destroy the pacifist leader at the conference (the Lansbury speech). Paraphrase only.
- **1937, Chairman of the TUC** (GK): rearmament against the left.

## Act III: this camp's angle

- **Munich, 1938, from outside the House:** the union's line.
- **13 May 1940:** Minister of Labour without a seat. Wandsworth unopposed.
- **1940–45, manpower:** the Essential Work Order; women into industry; 48,000 conscripts down the mines. The Bevin Boys scene uses the pit route's memories.
- **February 1943:** furious with Labour's own rebels over Beveridge.
- **1945, the Foreign Office** (not the Treasury he expected; GK).
- **The US loan, 1945:** Gallagher among the 22 rebels. The player must whip his own protégé.
- **January 1947, the bomb:** "a Union Jack on top of it" is a famous reported remark (GK). Paraphrase only.
- **Palestine, 1945–48.** A hard subject in full: immigration limits, the *Exodus*, the withdrawal.
- **Marshall Aid, Brussels, NATO, 1947–49.**
- **Windrush, 1948** (Isaacson as Minister of Labour): what the minister tells the House.
- **July 1949, the dock strike:** troops into the docks against your own members.
- **April 1951:** death in office (Bevin died 14 April 1951). Health strand.
